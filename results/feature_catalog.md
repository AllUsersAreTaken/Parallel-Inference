# N=8 stream population: feature catalog

Generated by `harness/feature_audit.py` (no GPU, read-only inputs). Data: `results/probs_probe30.json` + `data/_probs_pNN.jsonl`, `results/hidden_probe30.json` + `data/_hid_pNN.jsonl`.

**Population.** 32 problems x 8 streams, llama 3.2 3B, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, seed 1234, one RTX 4060. 55567 emitted-token rows, 8099 cross-stream consensus rows. Answer groups: UR=12, SP=19, UW=1.

**Read me first.** n=32 problems, one model, one seed, one machine. At n=32 the 95% band for a zero correlation is roughly |r|<0.35, so `|r|<0.2` means *no detectable association*, not proven independence. Effect sizes are reported instead of p-values. Nothing was tuned to improve a result; the one non-obvious alignment below is forced by the dump and is asserted in code.

## 0. Alignment (important)

`probs` step `t` is the distribution that emitted token `t` (0-based). Hidden sidecar `h` step `k` is captured on the same tick as `probs` step `k`: it is the state *after* token `k-1` that produces token `k`. The consensus row `c` step `t` is the consensus of `h` step `t+1`, so it is **contemporaneous with probs step t+1**, not step `t`. The loader asserts `c[t].cos_mean == mean(h[t+1].cmean)` over live streams for all 8099 consensus rows (holds to <2e-4).

Consequence: naive `corr(c[t], agreement[t])` manufactures a +1-step "hidden lead". The aligned series below is `Cal[t] = c[t-1]` (the state that produces token `t`). Section 1 reports both; the naive peak is a dump artifact, not a population property.

## 1. Lead-lag: does hidden consensus lead token agreement?

**Method.** Per run, token agreement `A[t]` = modal token fraction among live streams; hidden consensus `C[t] = c[t].cos_mean`; aligned `Cal[t] = C[t-1]`. `r(k) = corr(Cal[t], A[t+k])`, k=-10..+10, Fisher-averaged over 32 runs. Variants: levels, first differences, 21-step moving-average detrend, all-8-live steps only. Positive k = hidden at `t` vs agreement later.

The hidden drop is largest *after* the token drop, consistent with tokens driving representations rather than the reverse.

**Verdict.** No hidden lead. The highest-value question gets a clean negative after fixing the alignment; consensus and agreement are contemporaneous outputs of the same trajectory divergence.

## 4. Convergence dynamics

**Method.** `A[t]` and `Cal[t]` over `n>=2` steps. Re-convergence = `A<1` at some step and `A==1` later. Epochs = run-length encoding of `A==1`/`A<1`, split by mean live count. Slopes are OLS on relative trace position, per run. The full-probs final step (`n=1`) is excluded: it trivially has A=1 and produces a false 100% re-convergence rate.

- Re-convergence (n>=2): **2/32 runs**.
- `A==1` epochs at mean n>=6: n=22, median 2, p90 5, max 16 steps.
- `A==1` epochs at mean n<6: n=21, median 1, p90 4, max 11 steps (survivor pairs; not general re-convergence).
- `A<1` epochs: n=58, median 165 steps (once streams split they stay split for most of the trace).

| series | full n>=2 median slope | frac neg | n=8 prefix | frac neg | 2nd half n=8 | frac neg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| token agreement A | 0.004 | 50.0% | -0.268 | 100.0% | -0.007 | 59.4% |
| hidden consensus C | -0.179 | 100.0% | -0.227 | 100.0% | -0.018 | 81.2% |

Slope distributions (per run, relative trace position): A full n>=2 p10/p90 = -0.102/0.154; C full p10/p90 = -0.272/-0.081; A n=8 p10/p90 = -0.408/-0.143; C n=8 p10/p90 = -0.345/-0.138.

**Shape.** Both series fall fast while all 8 streams are live (median A slope -0.268, C slope -0.227, 32/32 A-negative, 32/32 C-negative). Then token agreement **saturates at a floor** (2nd-half A slope -0.007, 59.4% negative = flat) while hidden consensus keeps a slow drift down (2nd-half C slope -0.018, 81.2% negative); r(A,C) falls from 0.786 in the first half to 0.217 in the second. This is a real two-phase pattern, but part of it is **censoring**: A is a discrete modal fraction with a floor near 1/n, so it cannot keep falling once streams use different words. Do not read the late A plateau as semantic convergence.

The ~0.2 agreement floor is **not chance**: observed pairwise token equality is 0.068, versus ~1e-5 under a uniform 128k vocabulary. The floor is a shared function-word/formatting vocabulary, not random collisions.

Hidden consensus epochs: `C>=0.99` covers 64 steps (0.8%), 23 epochs, median 2, p90 5, max 15, mean relative position 0.05; `C>=0.999` covers 43 steps, 16 epochs, mean relative position 0.01. Hidden consensus is a **brief early phenomenon** (first ~5 steps), not a recurring late state.

**Composition warning.** n=8 covers 66.2% of consensus steps; 94.0% of last-decile steps have n<=4 and the last step has n=1. Any trace-position profile without an n filter shows spurious late re-convergence.

## 5. Variance decomposition: across streams vs across steps

**Method.** Balanced all-8-live prefix. Two-way layout `H[s,t]`: SS_step = S*sum_t(mean_s H - grand)^2, SS_stream = L*sum_s(mean_t H - grand)^2, residual = remainder (stream x step interaction + noise). Also mean_t Var_s(H) vs mean_s Var_t(H). Global hesitation = every live stream has H above a threshold.

- Median variance shares: **step 0.211, stream 0.011, residual 0.771** (p10-p90 step 0.171-0.273, stream 0.003-0.040). The additive step effect is ~19x the additive stream effect, but the stream x step interaction is ~4x the step effect: entropy is mostly idiosyncratic, not a stable per-stream or per-position property.
- Mean cross-stream variance / mean cross-step within-stream variance: median **0.902** (p10-p90 0.829-0.991). Neither factor dominates; the bulk is idiosyncratic stream x step variation. Stable stream identity explains ~1% of entropy variance. This decomposition uses the all-8-live prefix only; the late n<8 phase is not represented.

**Global hesitation windows:**

| threshold | steps | share | windows | runs | median dur | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H>0.693 | 35 | 0.7% | 32 | 26/32 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| H>1.000 | 4 | 0.1% | 4 | 4/32 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| H>1.500 | 0 | 0.0% | 0 | 0/32 | n/a | n/a | 0 |

At the repo's 1-nat threshold simultaneous hesitation is essentially absent: 4 all-live steps (0.1%), duration 1. At log-2 it is still rare and never lasts more than 2 steps. For contrast, **global confidence** (all H<0.05) covers 252/5358 all-live steps (4.7%): the population is much more likely to be jointly confident than jointly uncertain.

## 6. Sampler fidelity

**Method.** Per emitted token reconstruct an effective distribution from `(p1, H)`: top token `p1`, remainder uniform over `K-1` effective tokens with `K` chosen to match `H`. This gives a temperature-scaled top probability `p1T`, collision `sum q^2`, and `P(all 8 iid draws equal)`. Compare with the observed argmax rate (`exp(lp)/p1 > 0.999`) and with step-level token agreement. The reconstruction is rough (no rank-2 mass, no top-p truncation); it is used for order of magnitude.

| predicted top-1 bin (T=1) | n | observed argmax | mean pred T=1 | mean pred T=0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0.00,0.30) | 207 | 0.401 | 0.257 | 0.456 |
| [0.30,0.50) | 2224 | 0.552 | 0.423 | 0.658 |
| [0.50,0.70) | 5576 | 0.726 | 0.599 | 0.829 |
| [0.70,0.85) | 4685 | 0.915 | 0.779 | 0.967 |
| [0.85,0.95) | 4754 | 0.979 | 0.905 | 0.996 |
| [0.95,1.01) | 37143 | 1.000 | 0.996 | 1.000 |

- Observed argmax rate **0.942**; predicted mean top-1 at T=1 **0.903**, at T=0.6 **0.963**. Per-stream sampling is broadly consistent with a temperature around 0.6 (not exact); no evidence of gross sampler infidelity.
- Observed pairwise token equality **0.068** vs shared-distribution prediction **0.974**. Observed fraction of steps with A==1 **1.3%** vs predicted P(all 8 same) **91.1%**. Under the null that all 8 streams sample one common distribution, token agreement would be an order of magnitude higher. The gap is **content divergence**, not sampling noise: each stream is locally confident but their modes differ.
- At step 0 (shared prompt) observed agreement is 0.871 vs shared-null 0.586 (n=32). The observed value is *higher* than the reconstruction predicts, consistent with top-p/top-k truncation removing tail mass and with the shared prefix; it is not evidence of correlated RNG.

**Verdict.** The population is not sampling one shared distribution, but that is the expected consequence of independent sampling at near-ties compounding into different contexts. Per-stream top-1 rates are in the right range for T=0.6.

## 8. Hidden-state norm

**Method.** Norm = `h.norm` (last-layer embedding after final norm), one row per emitted token. Distribution over all 55567 rows; token-identity eta^2 = between-token SS / total SS for tokens with >=20 occurrences; per-trace slope before and after subtracting token means; within-run correlations; top |delta| spikes.

- Distribution: mean **88.44**, sd **3.56**, p1/p5/p50/p95/p99 = 73.4 / 79.9 / 89.6 / 91.1 / 91.5, min 63.6, max 92.3. EOS rows have mean norm 91.1, at the high end, not a low-norm special case.
- **Token identity eta^2 = 0.66** (378 common tokens cover 92% of rows). The single space token (id 220) alone accounts for **53.0%** of norm variance; its mean norm is **79.3** vs **89.2** for non-space (d=-2.6). Punctuation and newline-adjacent tokens are also low. Norm is mostly a property of *which token was emitted*, not of the context.

## 9. Timing

**Method.** Straggler = max completion / median completion per run; within-run correlation of log completion with log generated length; first/last finisher correctness vs the other streams; bootstrap over problems for the first-finisher difference.

- Straggler ratio: median **1.17**, p90 **1.42**, max **1.63**.
- Within-run corr(log completion, log generated) = **0.999** (median). Timing is essentially trace length; not an independent axis.
- First-finisher correctness **0.84** vs second **0.72** vs last **0.66**. First minus rest = **+0.143** (bootstrap 95% CI +0.013..+0.277); first minus second d=0.30. Within-run rank-correctness r=-0.125. First finisher is also the shortest trace in 100.0% of runs.
- First-finisher stream id counts: 1:2, 2:4, 3:7, 4:1, 5:3, 6:2, 7:10, 8:3. No fixed stream; this is a length/quality effect, not a stream-order effect.
- First finisher vs the other 7 (paired): entropy +0.001 (d=0.01); norm +0.096 (d=0.41); cmean +0.024 (d=0.79); generated -56.710 (d=-1.82).

**Interpretation.** The ~14 pp first-finisher advantage is suggestive (bootstrap CI just excludes 0) and fits the cross-run pattern that longer traces have lower answer agreement. With n=32 and one seed it is not robust enough to build on without replication.

## 10. Other structure that stands out

### 10.1 End-aligned answer convergence (the headline)

Absolute-step agreement is the wrong alignment for the answer: streams end at different lengths, so the shared suffix is compared at different indices. Aligning on the **end** of each trace (`k` = tokens from the end; k=0 is EOS) exposes a sharp convergence:

|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | 0.781 | 0.958 | 0.664 |
| 2 | 0.875 | 0.906 | 0.855 |
| 3 | 0.910 | 0.917 | 0.908 |
| 4 | 0.934 | 0.958 | 0.914 |
| 5 | 0.930 | 0.958 | 0.914 |
| 6 | 0.918 | 0.948 | 0.901 |
| 7 | 0.750 | 0.802 | 0.717 |
| 8 | 0.613 | 0.740 | 0.539 |
| 9 | 0.668 | 0.771 | 0.612 |
| 10 | 0.652 | 0.729 | 0.612 |
| 11 | 0.488 | 0.552 | 0.461 |
| 12 | 0.477 | 0.521 | 0.461 |

Agreement rises from 0.433 at 10-20 tokens before the end to **0.891 in the last 6 tokens** (+0.458 absolute, ~2x). The rise is the templated answer suffix (`\n\nThe answer is: X`), not the reasoning. The answer value token itself (k=1) is 0.781 overall: 0.958 in UR runs but only 0.664 in SP runs. `suffix_agree` and `answer_token_agree` correlate r=0.504 and r=0.877 with answer agreement; `suffix_agree` vs end-of-reasoning agreement is only r=0.210, so suffix convergence is largely independent of reasoning convergence.

**Interpretation.** The true late behaviour is **format convergence on top of content divergence**. Start-aligned agreement alone shows divergence everywhere and misses this. Caveat: the last token is EOS (always shared, excluded from the summary) and much of the rest is a fixed template, so this is not evidence that reasoning converges.

### 10.4 Answer-marker timing

The last `The answer is` marker occurs a median of 7 tokens before each stream's end (n=242 streams); 31/32 runs still have >=4 streams live at the median marker step (median 5 live). The answer phase is short and the live set is already shrinking, which is why end alignment is the usable handle.

## Ranked top-5 features

Criterion: size of effect, how much it changes the picture, and whether it survives the obvious composition/alignment controls. Effect sizes, not p-values.

1. **End-aligned answer convergence on top of reasoning divergence.** Last-6-token agreement 0.891 vs 0.433 ten-to-twenty back (+0.458, ~2x); answer-token agreement 0.781 (UR 0.958, SP 0.664); r(answer-token, answer agreement)=0.877 while r(suffix, end-of-reasoning)=0.210. Caveat: mostly the templated suffix, last token is EOS, invisible under absolute-step alignment. Not semantic convergence.
2. **Confidence and consensus are two nearly independent axes.** Run-level r(token agreement, mean entropy)=0.012, r(hidden cos, mean entropy)=0.397; within-run r(A,H)=-0.040; meanwhile r(A,C)=0.522. A and C disagree across their medians in 38.1% of steps. Caveat: entropy and margin are nearly collinear (|r|>0.98), so "confidence" is one axis; one seed, 32 problems.
3. **Token agreement saturates; hidden consensus keeps drifting.** First-half all-8-live slopes A=-0.268, C=-0.227, r(A,C)=0.786; second half A=-0.007 (flat), C=-0.018 (81.2% of runs still negative), r(A,C)=0.217. Caveat: A is censored by its discrete floor near 1/n, so part of the late dissociation is a measurement-floor artifact; the robust part is the two-phase shape.
4. **No hidden-state lead; the apparent +1 lead is an alignment artifact.** Naive join peaks at lag +1 (r=0.536); after shifting c by one step the peak is lag 0; detrended r(0)=0.250, r(+1)=0.058. Partial C[t]->A[t+1]|A[t]=0.296 vs reverse A[t]->C[t+1]|C[t]=0.308. This is a clean, deterministic negative and it corrects a natural first read of the dump.
5. **Hidden norm is mostly token identity, not context.** eta^2(token id)=0.66 (66% of norm variance); the space token alone is 53.0% of variance (mean 79.3 vs 89.2 non-space, d=-2.6); raw per-trace slope -1.29 but +0.99 after removing token means. Top norm spikes are space-token transitions. Caveat: descriptive, but the effect is large and would silently corrupt any norm-based convergence feature.

### Honourable mention / classification

- **Suggestive, not robust:** first-finisher advantage (+14.3 pp, bootstrap CI +1.3..+27.7 pp, d=0.30 vs second; n=32, confounded with trace length).
- **Robust-looking but n=32/single-seed:** end-aligned convergence, two axes, two-phase saturation, norm token identity.
- **Noise-level / negative:** per-stream identity (spread ratios 0.82-1.34, all below permutation p95); global hesitation (4/5358 all-live steps at H>1.0); hidden lead (no positive-lag peak after alignment); sampler infidelity (argmax rate 0.942 between T=1 0.903 and T=0.6 0.963).

## Caveats

- n=32 problems, one model, one seed, one machine; no extrapolation.
- Steps within a run are strongly autocorrelated; all step-level "n" values overstate independent sample size. Run-level or clustered bootstrap is the relevant unit.
- The last decile of each trace has a shrinking live set; 94.0% of last-decile steps have n<=4. Start-aligned profiles without an n filter are biased.
- `A` is a discrete modal fraction (multiples of 1/n) and is censored near its floor; `C` is compressed near 1 for identical text.
- The sampler reconstruction in section 6 is a rough three-moment model, not the true full-vocabulary distribution.
- End-aligned agreement is post-hoc in the sense that alignment is a choice; it is justified by the traces ending at the answer, but it should be replicated on another seed before being treated as a stable population law.
