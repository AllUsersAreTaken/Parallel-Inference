VERDICT
  1) Large shared trunk to bank?
     NO all-8 trunk: median first fork after 0.0 shared token(s) (p90=2.0).
     The all-8 trunk is 0-2 tokens for 90% of problems; by 25% of the trace the
     population is ~8 distinct prefixes (mean 7.9), i.e. 8 separate chains.
     Prefix collapse saves a median 4.24% of token positions (max 16.13%); a few
     problems keep a 6/8 modal branch for ~20 tokens, but that is modest, not large.
  2) Fork timing?
     FRONT-LOADED. Median fork depth is 4.0% of trace length (p90=11.5%).
     After that the trie is a set of long singleton chains; 57.4% of fork
     children are 1/8 branches.
  3) Correctness monotone in branch mass?
     NO increasing trend: slightly decreasing with mass (weak), delta=-0.0403, spearman=-0.0806.
     Decision statistic is correctness density; P(any correct) is size-biased.
     Low-mass branches are not systematically less correct. The high-mass
     bucket is only 39 child instances and the rows are nested, so treat the
     flat/slightly-negative point estimate as weak, not a reliable negative law.
  4) Tail-only-correct problems (G3): 3  ids=[14, 29, 32]
  5) Decision rule (monotone means monotone-increasing in mass):
     G3>0 and correctness is not increasing with branch mass -> WORTH A GPU SESSION.
     Tail cases exist and mass does not identify them in advance. This is a weak
     structural green light, not a demonstrated capability: the monotonicity
     evidence is flat, n=32, and one of the three tail cases (q32) is a selected
     boost. The next step is a cheap targeted amplification experiment, not a sweep.

CAVEATS
  * n=32 problems, 8 streams, one model (llama3.2-3b), one seed (1234), T=0.6/top_p=0.95.
  * Branch masses are integer counts / 8 (multiples of 1/8); with 8 streams the
    smallest non-zero mass is 0.125 and estimates are coarse.
  * Forks are token-level and nested: the same stream contributes to many fork
    children, so the monotonicity table is descriptive, not an independent-sample test.
  * Branch correctness density uses one sampled continuation per stream. It is not
    P(correct | more samples from that prefix), which is what an amplification
    experiment would need; this analysis cannot estimate that conditional.
  * The trie merges exact token-id prefixes only. Different text that tokenizes
    differently, or reasoning paths that re-converge after diverging, are counted
    as separate; token_saving is an upper bound on prefix-collapse savings and a
    lower bound on any broader (non-prefix) reuse.
  * Traces are the sidecar's emitted tokens, including EOS (tok 128009) where the
    stream finished by EOS; EOS sits under each terminal prefix and merges nothing.
  * 12/32 problems are all-correct, 1/32 all-wrong, and 19/32 have both classes.
    Only the both-class problems can inform correctness-vs-mass; the mass table
    excludes all-correct/all-wrong problems, while fork-depth stats include all.
  * selection is 30 length quantiles + 2 boosts chosen as unanimous-wrong in the
    n=100 run (q31, q32). q32 is one of the tail-only cases, so G3 is inflated by
    the boost design; the random quantile subset alone contributes q14 and q29 (2/30).
  * answers are the probe's canonicalized answers; correctness is exact string
    equality after that canonicalization. No semantic/numeric tolerance is added.
  * This is structural analysis of token sequences, not a wall-clock or GPU measurement.
